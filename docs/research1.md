# The Instruction Set That Wasn't Designed: Will Mechanistic Interpretability Become the Software Abstraction Layer of Transformers?

## TL;DR
- **Probably not as a single clean "transformer ISA," but yes as a partial, leaky, multi-layer abstraction stack that is already becoming load-bearing in safety engineering.** The CPU analogy is generative but breaks at its load-bearing joint: CPU abstraction boundaries were *designed and enforced* top-down, whereas transformer structure is *found* by SGD in a continuous space with superposition actively fighting clean interfaces. Tracr proves the map from high-level program to weights *can* exist; it does not prove SGD-trained models *have* one.
- **The strongest evidence for a durable abstraction layer is not activation-space SAEs (which took heavy hits in 2025) but the pivot to weight/parameter-space decomposition and interpretability-by-design.** APD/SPD (Apollo/Goodfire), OpenAI's weight-sparse transformers (Gao et al., Nov 2025), and the crosscoder/universality results are the real candidates for a "static" layer; SAEs increasingly look like a useful-but-not-canonical descriptive tool after Kantamneni et al., Heap et al., and GDM's own deprioritization.
- **The right abstraction layer may not have been invented yet, and it may be plural.** Circuits, belief-state geometry (computational mechanics), and developmental/SLT phase-structure are non-reducible descriptions at different levels — closer to Marr's three levels or Anderson's "More Is Different" than to a compiler stack. The dynamical-systems view (attractors, Lyapunov exponents, transfer operators) is a live and under-exploited fourth candidate.

## Key Findings

1. **The analogy is not a loose metaphor — it is the field's founding self-conception, made explicit by Chris Olah.** In his 2022 essay he writes that "mechanistic interpretability seeks to reverse engineer neural networks, similar to how one might reverse engineer a compiled binary computer program… neural network parameters are in some sense a binary computer program which runs on one of the exotic virtual machines we call a neural network architecture. This is actually quite a deep analogy," complete with an explicit correspondence table (parameters↔binary, architecture↔VM/processor, activations↔program state/memory, feature direction↔variable/memory location). The "instruction set" framing is native, not imposed by outside commentators.

2. **Tracr is the crux exhibit — and it cuts against the "discovered ISA" reading.** Lindner et al. (2023) built a genuine compiler from RASP (Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav, 2021) to transformer weights, translating RASP→craft→weights "analogous to how programming languages are first translated to assembly then to machine code." But Tracr shows the abstraction stack is *constructible*, not that trained models *instantiate* it. OpenAI's own framing (2025) is careful here: with Tracr "the interpretability of the resulting weights is a property of the compilation pipeline rather than something the model acquired from a training objective." Friedman et al.'s "Transformer Programs" run the arrow the other way (train models that decompile to programs). Both are existence proofs for the map; neither shows a naturally-trained frontier model has a clean one.

3. **Superposition is the central structural obstruction to a clean instruction set, and it is not going away.** Elhage et al.'s Toy Models of Superposition establishes that networks represent more features than dimensions by tolerating interference — the opposite of a privileged, one-variable-per-register memory layout. Olah himself flags polysemantic neurons as "a really tricky problem." This is why the field's frontier has moved from neurons to features to (now) *parameter components*.

4. **The 2024–2026 SAE reckoning materially weakened the activation-feature layer as an ISA candidate.** Kantamneni, Engels, Rajamanoharan, Tegmark & Nanda ("Are Sparse Autoencoders Useful? A Case Study in Sparse Probing," ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.16681) tested SAE probes against logistic regression, KNN, XGBoost, MLP, and PCA across 113 binary datasets in four hard regimes, and found "SAE probes underperform the baseline of logistic regression in each regime when taking the mean across datasets." Heap, Lawson, Farnik & Aitchison ("Automated Interpretability Metrics Do Not Distinguish Trained and Random Transformers," arXiv 2501.17727) found SAEs trained on *randomly initialized* transformers produce similar auto-interp and reconstruction scores to trained ones. A Google DeepMind team publicly deprioritized fundamental SAE research after SAEs underperformed linear probes on OOD harmful-intent detection. Feature absorption (Chanin et al.), feature splitting, non-uniqueness across seeds (Paulo & Belrose), and Spolsky-style leaks are now documented failure modes.

5. **The most credible "static layer" candidates in 2025–2026 are weight-based.** Apollo's Attribution-based Parameter Decomposition (Bushnaq, Braun, Hex, Mendel, Sharkey; arXiv 2501.14926, Jan 2025) explicitly abandons the "activations→features→circuits" pipeline, decomposing *parameters* into mechanistic components optimized for faithfulness, minimality, and simplicity — recovering ground-truth mechanisms in superposition in toy settings. Goodfire's Stochastic Parameter Decomposition scales the idea. OpenAI's weight-sparse transformers (Gao, Rajaram, Coxon, Govande, Baker, Mossing; arXiv 2511.13653) force ~1-in-1000 weights nonzero and recover fully reverse-engineered circuits — the single/double-quote-matching circuit is just **12 nodes and 9 edges** — with circuits "~16× smaller than dense models at matched pretraining loss," and capability and interpretability dialable independently. But a replication (Anthropic Fellows) found these circuits can be "interpretable yet unfaithful," and, in the authors' own words, "scaling sparse models beyond tens of millions of nonzero parameters while preserving interpretability remains a challenge."

6. **Universality — the empirical precondition for a *transferable* ISA — is real but partial.** Olah's universality hypothesis has support: induction heads recur; Gurnee et al. ("Universal Neurons in GPT2 Language Models," arXiv 2401.12181), computing pairwise neuron correlations over 100M tokens across five seeds, "find that 1-5% of neurons are universal"; crosscoders (Lindsey et al., 2024) and SAE-feature-space studies show substantial cross-model feature alignment. But Anthropic's own crosscoder-diffing update reports that model-exclusive features are systematically *more* polysemantic (a capacity-competition artifact), and cross-architecture diffing remains hard. Universality is strong enough to make transfer plausible, too weak to guarantee a stable shared instruction set today.

7. **Attribution graphs are decompilation of an artifact that never had source — and Anthropic quantifies how leaky this is.** In "Circuit Tracing" and "On the Biology of a Large Language Model" (2025), cross-layer transcoders match the model's next-token output in ~50% of tested prompts; and, in the authors' words, "though it's difficult to quantify precisely, we've found that our attribution graphs provide us with satisfying insight for about a quarter of the prompts we've tried… even in our successful case studies, the discoveries we highlight here only capture a small fraction of the mechanisms of the model." Attention patterns are *frozen* (the QK-circuits — the "why" — are outside the explanation), "error nodes" absorb unexplained computation as bias-like dark matter, and reconstruction errors compound across layers. This is exactly Spolsky's Law of Leaky Abstractions with the leak rate measured.

8. **The neuroscience precedent (Jonas & Kording 2017) is the sharpest warning.** They ran standard neuroscience analyses on a simulated MOS 6502 (3,510 transistors) running Donkey Kong, Space Invaders, and Pitfall — a system whose ground truth is fully known — and concluded: "We find that many measures are surprisingly similar between the brain and the processor but that our results do not lead to a meaningful understanding of the processor. The analysis can not produce the hierarchical understanding of information processing that most students of electrical engineering obtain." The lesson for mech interp: bottom-up statistical analysis of a known computational artifact can yield plausible, publishable, and *wrong or shallow* stories. This directly motivates the field's ground-truth-benchmark turn (Tracr, weight-sparse models as testbeds) and the "interpretability illusions" literature (Bolukbasi et al.; Makelov et al.; the subspace-activation-patching illusion).

9. **Interp has produced genuine load-bearing engineering value that black-box methods could not — but narrowly.** Anthropic's auditing-game (Marks et al., 2025) showed interpretability tools helped teams find a deliberately hidden training objective, though success leaned heavily on training-data access. Feature steering, refusal directions, and activation-based probes/monitors are in or near production, and the discovery of evaluation-awareness features is a concrete safety-relevant find. But on many downstream tasks (probing, steering) simple baselines match or beat interp, so the honest verdict is: interp wins where the task is *discovering unknown concepts and auditing internal structure*, not where it is *acting on known concepts*.

10. **Competing abstraction layers exist and may be non-reducible.** Computational mechanics (Shai et al., NeurIPS 2024) shows transformers linearly embed the *belief-state geometry* — even fractal Mess3 structure — of the data-generating process in the residual stream, a representation-geometry ISA grounded in optimal prediction. Developmental interpretability / SLT (Hoogland, Murfet, van Wingerden; Timaeus) offers a *phase-transition* layer indexed by the local learning coefficient. The dynamical-systems view — the reader's own investment — is a live fourth candidate: looped transformers as fixed-point/iterated maps (Yang et al., ICLR 2024; Gatmiry et al.), Lyapunov/chaos analyses of reasoning, and transfer-operator/attractor accounts of generation. These are different *levels* (Marr), not competing hypotheses at one level.

## Details

### The analogy, built carefully

The engineering view and the mech-interp view genuinely are two descriptions of one artifact, and the CPU abstraction stack is the natural reference class. An electrical engineer sees transistors and gates; a computer architect sees caches, pipelines, ALUs, and an instruction decoder; a compiler writer sees SSA graphs and passes. Each level is *real*, *predictive*, and *usable without understanding the level below* — the defining properties of a good abstraction. Olah's framing maps mech interp onto exactly this: reverse-engineering a "binary program" (weights) running on "an exotic virtual machine" (the architecture), where the central task is recovering the "variables" (features) so that the "instructions" (parameters connecting features) become legible. Zoom In (Olah et al., 2020) gave the three load-bearing claims — features as the fundamental unit, circuits as features wired by weights, and universality across models — that a would-be ISA needs.

The reason the analogy is *seductive* is that it has a genuine constructive witness. RASP (Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav) is a human-readable programming model for transformer computation; Tracr (Lindner et al.) is a real compiler from RASP through an intermediate representation ("craft," linear algebra on named residual-stream subspaces) down to Haiku transformer weights. If you can *compile* a high-level program into transformer weights, then at least some transformer weight configurations are the image of a clean abstraction stack. This is the single strongest point in favor of "the ISA is real."

### Where the analogy breaks — and it breaks at the joint that matters

A CPU's abstraction boundaries were *designed*. Someone decided that the byte is the unit, that the L1/L2 boundary exists, that the ISA is the contract between compiler and silicon, and *enforced* those boundaries with modular hardware and specifications. The abstraction is clean because human engineers paid an optimization cost to keep it clean (chip area, verification effort). Transformers have no such pressure. SGD minimizes loss in a continuous parameter space; nothing in the objective rewards clean interfaces, modularity, or one-concept-per-direction layouts. On the contrary, superposition shows the optimizer actively *prefers* to overload directions to pack more features than dimensions — the exact opposite of the CPU's "one thing per byte" discipline that Olah notes makes reverse engineering tractable.

This is why Tracr cuts *against* the strong reading even as it supports the weak one. As OpenAI's 2025 weight-sparse paper puts it, in Tracr the interpretability is "a property of the compilation pipeline rather than something the model acquired from a training objective." The map exists; SGD does not follow it. The empirical modularity literature confirms the tension: Filan, Hod et al. find trained-and-pruned MLPs are *somewhat* more clusterable than random-but-same-weight-distribution controls — modularity is present but weak and largely a byproduct of pruning/sparsity, not a strong learned prior. Clusterability studies and the "one network fits all?" line conclude that SGD does not, on its own, deliver the crisp module boundaries an engineered system has.

So the honest statement is: **the abstraction stack is partly discovered and would have to be partly *imposed*.** Which is precisely why the 2025–2026 frontier is interpretability-by-design.

### The SAE reckoning: the activation-feature layer is descriptive, not (yet) canonical

For two years the field's best guess at the "variable" layer was the SAE feature. The 2024–2026 evidence forces a downgrade. Kantamneni et al. tested SAE probes against logistic regression, KNN, XGBoost, MLP, and PCA across 113 datasets in four hard regimes and found SAEs do not consistently win — "SAE probes underperform the baseline of logistic regression in each regime when taking the mean across datasets." Heap, Lawson, Farnik & Aitchison showed the field's *evaluation metrics themselves* cannot distinguish SAEs on trained vs. randomly-initialized transformers — an alarming result for anyone hoping SAE interpretability reflects learned computation rather than architecture-plus-data statistics. Wu et al. found SAEs underperform on steering; a Google DeepMind team wrote that "obvious non-interp baselines outperformed SAEs" and publicly deprioritized fundamental SAE research. Internal pathologies — feature absorption (a latent for "starts with S" silently failing on absorbed cases), feature splitting, seed-dependent non-uniqueness (Paulo & Belrose), irreducible reconstruction error with predictable structure (Engels et al.) — are exactly the "leaks" Spolsky warned about, and they compound.

The rebuttal (the "use SAEs to discover *unknown* concepts, not to act on *known* ones" position) is the right steelman: SAEs remain the best tool for open-ended feature *discovery*, where the baseline (you have to already name the concept to probe for it) doesn't apply. But a discovery tool that you cannot canonicalize, that is non-unique across seeds, and whose quality metrics don't distinguish signal from architecture, is not yet an instruction set. It is a microscope with chromatic aberration.

### The weight-based turn: the real ISA candidate

The deepest response to superposition is to stop decomposing activations and decompose *parameters*. Apollo's APD (Bushnaq, Braun, Hex, Mendel, Sharkey) reframes "mechanism space" as parameter space and searches for a decomposition that is faithful (sums to the real weights), minimal (few components active per input), and simple (low-rank / short description). In toy models it recovers features from superposition, separates compressed computations, and identifies cross-layer distributed representations — the things that defeat activation-first methods. Lee Sharkey's framing is telling: the team came to think "decomposing network activations layer by layer into features and connecting those features up into circuits… may not be the way to go." Goodfire's Stochastic Parameter Decomposition (SPD) is the scalable successor.

OpenAI's weight-sparse transformers (Gao et al., Nov 2025) attack the problem from the training side: constrain roughly 1 in 1000 weights to be nonzero, and the surviving connectivity graph is thin enough that circuits for narrow Python tasks can be fully reverse-engineered and validated as necessary and sufficient by mean-ablation. The single/double-quote-matching circuit reduces to just **12 nodes and 9 edges**, and circuits come out "~16× smaller than dense models at matched pretraining loss," with capability and interpretability dialable independently. This is the closest thing yet to "interpretability by design," and it directly answers the "discovered vs. imposed" question: **impose the sparsity prior and the ISA appears.** The caveats are serious and named: an Anthropic-Fellows replication found weight-sparse circuits can be "interpretable yet unfaithful" to the model's true computation, and the authors themselves state that "scaling sparse models beyond tens of millions of nonzero parameters while preserving interpretability remains a challenge." But as an existence proof that architectural priors can manufacture a clean-ish layer, it is the most important result of the period.

### Decompilation without source, measured

Anthropic's attribution graphs are the field's most ambitious "decompiler." The disanalogy with real decompilation is exact and important: a decompiler recovers source from an artifact that *had* source and a known compiler; attribution graphs reverse-engineer an artifact that never had source and whose "compiler" is SGD. Anthropic's own numbers quantify the resulting leak: cross-layer transcoders reproduce the model's next-token output in ~50% of tested prompts; attribution graphs yield "satisfying insight for about a quarter of the prompts we've tried"; even the showcased case studies "only capture a small fraction of the mechanisms of the model." Structurally, attention patterns are frozen (so the QK "why" is excluded — in the acronym case, a token's real influence "through influencing attention patterns… our method does not explain"), and "error nodes" carry the unexplained residual as bias-like dark matter that compounds across layers. The biology framing ("microscope," "AI biology," organisms "grown" not "built") is not decoration — it is an admission that the object is closer to a found biological system than an engineered one, which is the anti-ISA position dressed as methodology.

### The neuroscience mirror

Jonas & Kording (2017) is the control experiment the whole field should keep on its desk. Take a system whose ground truth is *completely known* — a simulated MOS 6502, 3,510 transistors, running three games — and apply the standard toolkit (lesioning/ablation, tuning curves, dimensionality reduction, connectomics, spike-triggered analysis). Result: "many measures are surprisingly similar between the brain and the processor but… do not lead to a meaningful understanding of the processor," and the analysis "can not produce the hierarchical understanding of information processing that most students of electrical engineering obtain." Ablation finds transistors "necessary" for Donkey Kong but not Space Invaders and tempts you into "the Donkey Kong transistor" — a false-modularity story. The parallels to activation patching, ablation studies, and single-feature narratives in mech interp are uncomfortably tight. The field's best answer has been to build ground-truth testbeds (Tracr, weight-sparse models) and to catalog "interpretability illusions" (Bolukbasi et al.'s interpretability-illusion neurons; Makelov et al.; the subspace activation-patching illusion). That self-correction is real and healthy — but it exists precisely because bottom-up understanding of a known artifact is not guaranteed to converge on truth.

### The competing (and possibly non-reducible) layers

The reader's instinct that the right layer may not be circuits at all is well supported:

- **Representation geometry / computational mechanics.** Shai, Marzen, Teixeira, Gietelink Oldenziel & Riechers (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2405.15943) predict *a priori*, from the data-generating process, that a transformer will linearly embed the belief-state geometry (the Mixed-State Presentation) of that process in its residual stream — and confirm it, recovering the fractal Mess3 geometry via linear regression from a 64-dim residual stream, with belief states encoding information about the entire future beyond next-token prediction. This is an ISA at Marr's *representational/algorithmic* level that is *derived*, not reverse-engineered — arguably more principled than circuits because it makes falsifiable predictions before you look inside.

- **Developmental interpretability / SLT.** Hoogland, Murfet, van Wingerden and the Timaeus program index model structure by *training dynamics* — Bayesian phase transitions characterized by the local learning coefficient (an algebraic-geometric invariant of the loss landscape, from Watanabe's singular learning theory). The bet is that you can only carve a model at its joints if you watch the joints form. Their motivating examples are Olsson et al.'s induction-head phase change and Elhage et al.'s superposition. This is a *temporal* abstraction layer orthogonal to any static one, and its safety pitch is explicit: phase transitions are "exactly the points which are strategically crucial to detect," where extrapolation fails and dangerous capabilities crystallize.

- **Dynamical systems.** Autoregressive generation is literally a discrete-time iterated map on the residual stream. Looped transformers behave as fixed-point iterations that emulate multi-step (preconditioned) gradient descent for in-context learning (Yang, Lee, Nowak & Papailiopoulos, ICLR 2024; Gatmiry et al., 2024, arXiv 2410.08292) — with the sharp caveat that they "emulate the algorithm with the particular input distribution" they were trained on and fail OOD, carrying "an inherent error floor." Quasi-Lyapunov analyses treat reasoning as a chaotic system (concluding MLPs dominate the final output and that small initial perturbations propagate, i.e., positive-exponent regimes). Transfer-operator/Koopman and attractor-geometry accounts (2025–2026 preprints, not yet peer-reviewed) recast generation as delay-embedding + invariant-set forecasting, memory as basins, and hallucination as attractor mis-arbitration. This view is *under-exploited* in safety-relevant interp and is the most natural home for questions about generation stability, mode collapse, and reasoning chains — but its frontier is very recent and thinly peer-reviewed, so it should be flagged as promising-but-immature.

The precedent-from-other-sciences reading strongly favors *plurality*. Marr's three levels (computational/algorithmic/implementational), Dennett's stances, multiple realizability, and Anderson's "More Is Different" all say the same thing: for a sufficiently complex system, there is no single privileged description, and coarse-grained levels can be autonomous (predictive on their own terms) without being reducible in practice to the level below. Renormalization group is the one clean precedent for *principled* coarse-graining — and it is exactly what SLT/devinterp and computational mechanics are reaching for. Connectomics is the cautionary precedent: having the full wiring diagram of C. elegans did not hand anyone an understanding of its behavior, just as the 6502 netlist did not.

### Against the whole frame

The strongest counter-position deserves to be stated at full strength: mech-interp abstractions may be *epiphenomenal just-so stories* — post-hoc narratives that are plausible, publishable, and causally shallow. The evidence marshaled for this: SAE metrics that can't distinguish trained from random models; interpretability illusions that survived peer review; the routine failure of interp features to beat linear baselines; the ~25%-of-prompts honesty of attribution graphs; and the Jonas–Kording demonstration that the toolkit fails on a known artifact. Sharkey et al.'s "Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability" (TMLR 2025) concedes the epistemics directly: "conflating hypotheses with conclusions has regrettably been commonplace," and it is "often hard to distinguish faithful explanations of neural network components from merely plausible ones." On this view, top-down representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023) and black-box behavioral evaluation are simply *more useful per unit effort* — RepE reads and controls honesty/harmfulness/power-seeking directions without any circuit-level story, and its "Hopfieldian" (population-geometry) stance explicitly rejects the neuron/circuit frame. The extreme version — "interpretability may not be the right frame at all" — holds that what we want is *control and prediction of behavior*, which geometry and dynamics may deliver without ever yielding a human-readable instruction set.

I take this seriously but do not fully endorse it. The auditing-game and evaluation-awareness results show interp doing something black-box methods provably could not (surfacing a *hidden* objective the model was trained to conceal). The correct synthesis is that interp's value is currently *concentrated* in discovery/auditing and *thin* in acting-on-known-concepts, and that the abstraction layer, if it forms, will be built on weight/parameter structure and geometry rather than on activation-space SAE features.

### A concrete proposed stack — and where it leaks

If a "transformer ISA" forms, it will most plausibly look like this layered stack, with the leaks named:

- **weights** → **parameter components** (APD/SPD; leak: non-uniqueness of decomposition, faithfulness-vs-simplicity tradeoff)
- **parameter components** → **features** (SAEs/transcoders; leak: absorption, splitting, seed-dependence, irreducible reconstruction error)
- **features** → **circuits** (attribution graphs; leak: frozen attention/QK, error-node dark matter, ~25% insight rate)
- **circuits** → **algorithms** (RASP-like descriptions; leak: OOD brittleness, distribution-specific emulation)
- **algorithms** → **behaviors** (evals/steering; leak: eval-awareness, out-of-distribution generalization)

Every arrow is leaky in the Spolsky sense, and the leaks are not independent — reconstruction error at the feature layer compounds into circuit unfaithfulness, which compounds into wrong algorithmic stories. A usable engineering abstraction does not require zero leaks; it requires *budgeted, characterized* leaks. Today the leak budget is only partially quantified (Anthropic's ~50% / ~25% numbers are the honest exemplars) and mostly at the wrong layer (activations rather than weights).

## Recommendations

**For your own research program (published in circuit discovery, steering, refusal directions, jailbreak defense):**

1. **Rebase circuit-discovery work onto weight/parameter-space and sparse-by-design substrates.** The activation-SAE foundation is now empirically shaky for anything beyond concept *discovery*. Concretely: run your attribution-graph / active-circuit-discovery pipeline on OpenAI's released weight-sparse models and on APD/SPD decompositions as ground-truth-ish testbeds, and report *faithfulness* (mean-ablation necessity/sufficiency, perturbation consistency) as a first-class metric, not just plausibility. Benchmark to change your mind: if weight-sparse circuits reproduce your refusal-direction findings and dense-model SAE circuits don't, that is decisive evidence about which substrate is the real ISA.

2. **Treat refusal directions and jailbreak defenses as a dynamical-systems problem, not only a linear-direction problem.** Your investment in attractors/Lyapunov/bifurcations is well-placed and under-served. A concrete, publishable program: measure whether jailbreaks correspond to the generation trajectory crossing a *bifurcation* (a refusal basin losing stability) rather than merely to a refusal direction being suppressed; estimate quasi-Lyapunov exponents along refusal vs. compliance trajectories; test whether activation-based defenses work by *deepening the refusal attractor* vs. *steering across a separatrix*. This reframes "symmetry breaking" in refusal as a bifurcation phenomenon and could yield defenses that are robust because they are stability-based rather than direction-based.

3. **Adopt the "discover unknown, don't act on known" division explicitly.** For deployment-facing jailbreak defense, prefer strong linear probes / RepE-style monitors as the load-bearing classifier (they win on known concepts), and reserve SAEs/circuits for *finding* the unknown attack surface (novel jailbreak families, hidden objectives). Threshold: if a linear probe on raw activations matches your SAE-feature monitor on OOD attack detection, ship the probe and keep the SAE for red-teaming.

**Staged next steps for the field-level question:**

- *Near term (now–12 mo):* Establish faithfulness benchmarks with ground truth (Tracr, weight-sparse, APD toy models) as the standard evaluation for any "circuit." Treat the Heap et al. random-transformer control as a mandatory sanity check for any new feature/metric.
- *Mid term:* Push parameter decomposition (APD/SPD) and weight-sparse training toward the tens-of-millions→billions-of-nonzero-parameter regime; the benchmark that would change the verdict is a *faithful* weight-sparse or parameter-decomposed circuit at frontier-model scale.
- *Long term:* Pursue the plural stack explicitly — weights→parameter-components→features→circuits→algorithms→behaviors, cross-checked against belief-state geometry and phase-structure — and accept that leaks between layers are permanent (Spolsky) and must be *budgeted*, not eliminated.

**What would change these recommendations:** If a large-scale, seed-stable, faithful SAE canonicalization emerged (defeating absorption/splitting/non-uniqueness and beating baselines on acting-on-known-concepts), the activation-feature layer would be resurrected as the primary ISA. If weight-sparse/parameter-decomposition methods failed to scale past toy/narrow regimes, the "ISA is imposed, not discovered" route would close and the geometry/dynamics layers would become the only viable durable abstractions.

## Caveats

- **Anthropic's headline faithfulness numbers (~50% CLT output-match; ~25% of prompts yielding insight; "small fraction of mechanisms") are self-reported and explicitly hedged as "difficult to quantify precisely," not independently audited.** Treat them as honest lower-bound-ish self-assessments, not validated benchmarks.
- **Several dynamical-systems sources (attractor-geometry of memory, transfer-operator ICL, quasi-Lyapunov reasoning) are 2025–2026 arXiv preprints not yet peer-reviewed.** The load-bearing peer-reviewed dynamics results are the looped-transformer / iterated-map papers (ICLR 2024) and the belief-state-geometry paper (NeurIPS 2024).
- **"Universality" is measured heterogeneously** — Gurnee et al.'s 1–5% is a strict neuron-correlation criterion; SAE-feature-space alignment studies use looser representational-similarity measures; these are not the same claim and should not be pooled.
- **The SAE-critique literature and the SAE-defense literature are both active and partly talking past each other** (probing/steering benchmarks vs. discovery use-cases). I have taken the position that discovery is where SAEs retain value, but this is a judgment call, not settled consensus.
- **The CPU analogy itself may flatter the target.** A CPU is deterministic, discrete, and modular by construction; a transformer is continuous, superposed, stochastic in training, and shaped by data statistics. The analogy is most useful as a source of *disanalogies* — each place it breaks (designed vs. found interfaces, enforced vs. absent modularity, source-having vs. sourceless artifact) is a real research question, not a rhetorical flourish.
- **Some corroborating detail came from secondary summaries** (e.g., third-party write-ups of OpenAI's weight-sparse paper and Anthropic's 2026 interpretability work); the primary-source figures cited above (Gao et al.'s 12-node/9-edge circuit and 16× number, Kantamneni et al.'s per-regime finding, Gurnee et al.'s 1–5%, Jonas–Kording's conclusion, Anthropic's ~50%/~25%) were verified against the papers or the authors' own text.
