Programming the transformer

Can the internals of a transformer be treated the way a compiler treats a CPU — with variables, instructions, a debugger, and eventually a language a non-expert can program in? This project answers empirically: five preregistered experiments validate which layers of the hypothetical "abstraction stack" are real, and Loom, a prototype programming layer, is being built on the layers that survived.

1 · The question

Modern computing is a tower of abstractions: application → language → compiler → assembly → instruction set → silicon. Every layer was designed. A transformer has no such tower — it jumps from linear algebra straight to application, and the middle is missing:

The literature synthesis that seeded this project concluded the layer, if it exists, is plural and leaky — not one clean instruction set but several partial descriptions, each with a measurable failure fraction ("leak budget"). That claim was turned into five falsifiable hypotheses with numeric refutation thresholds, then tested on a single NVIDIA GB10 with small transformers whose ground truth we control.

Three auxiliary engines steered the work: a TRIZ contradiction analysis (interpretability ↔ capability; imposed ↔ discovered structure) generated the experiment variants; an active-inference selector ranked candidate experiments by expected information gain per GPU-minute; and a ralph loop re-entered every experiment until its verdict was grounded in a committed artifact — no result below was accepted on first pass.

E1 — Geometry: the layer that held

A 4-layer transformer trained on the Mess3 hidden-Markov process embeds the process's belief-state simplex in its residual stream, linearly readable at R² = 0.998. The decisive control: an untrained network already probes at 0.887 (random features memorize recent tokens), and the last-8-tokens baseline explains 0.967. The signal is the incremental R² beyond that window — 0.0314 trained vs 0.000326 untrained, a ~96× gap. The fractal geometry is visible by eye:

E2 — Circuits: real, discrete, and shortcut-prone

Induction (in-context copying) forms as a phase transition: prefix-matching attention rises to 0.607 inside 0.155 of training, co-timed with the in-context loss collapsing to near zero — and it lives in layer 2 only (0.607 vs 0.0734 in layer 1), the classic two-layer composition. The route was the finding: the first design used fixed-offset repetition and got perfect copying with zero induction attention — a positional shortcut. Randomizing the gap destroyed the shortcut and the true circuit appeared.

E3 & E4 — Features: descriptive, not canonical

E3 asked whether SAE quality metrics can even tell a trained model from a random one (they failed to in the literature). Here they separated at 22.5σ — but in the wrong direction: the random model reconstructs more easily (FVU 0.00524 vs 0.0524) because its activations are low-dimensional. The metric scores reconstructability, not learned structure. E4: on known concepts, a plain logistic probe on raw activations ties the SAE probe on belief-region (0.908 vs 0.907) and loses slightly on entropy (0.679 vs 0.72) — parity, not superiority, replicated at a gap of 0.001 ± 0.002.

E5 — Weights: undecidable as measured, and that is the result

Training at 80% weight sparsity kept 1 accuracy with a perfectly faithful extracted circuit — sparsity is nearly free. But the headline "4.8× smaller circuit" deflated on replication: the weight ratio (0.209), an independent edge-level ratio (0.209), and the imposed sparsity knob (q = 0.2) are the same number, with zero variance across seeds. Every size metric we built measures the hyperparameter, not the learned mechanism. The experiment now self-reports this (size_metrics_are_tautological) and its posterior is pinned at the 0.5 prior — passing an uninformative test is not evidence.

4 · What almost fooled us

Every positive result was one control away from being wrong. These four failure modes were each caught live, on systems whose ground truth we knew — which is precisely why toy models first:

The reservoir effect

An untrained network probed belief state at R² 0.887 — random features over recent tokens explain almost everything. Any probe result without an untrained control and a trivial-baseline comparison is unfounded.

The positional shortcut

Perfect in-context copying with zero induction attention: fixed-offset repetition is solvable from positional embeddings alone. A behavioral metric certified a capability the mechanism did not implement.

GPU non-determinism

Identical runs moved a headline accuracy by 7 points (0.786 → 0.857) — enough to flip a verdict. Seeding is not enough: the memory-efficient attention backward pass is non-deterministic; the math backend must be pinned before CUDA initializes.

The tautological metric

"Sparse circuits are 4.8× smaller" was the sparsity knob echoed back. The tell: zero variance across seeds. A result that reproduces perfectly may be reproducing a constant.

Standing rule, now enforced in code: the pipeline flags seed-invariant metrics, refuses to count tautological passes as evidence, verifies that support rules in code match the preregistered thresholds, and never lets the test suite write into real results.

5 · Loom — building the layer

Validation said which layers are load-bearing. Loom is the constructive step: a language in which a person who has never heard of gradients declares what the model must do, and a compiler either delivers a verified model or refuses with a diagnosis. The compiler's contract is the project's central lesson turned into software: no capability ships unverified.

6 · Steering — the open decisions

  • H5 needs a non-weight-counting ruler. Causal edge pruning or description length of the extracted algorithm; until then the weights layer stays at its prior.
  • Scale jump: re-run E3/E4 on a real LM (Pythia-160M) where the random-model control has its intended meaning; port a Loom monitor to it.
  • Loom expressiveness: v0 has 3 skill kinds, 2 control kinds, 1 monitor kind. The next kinds should come from what a real user asks for, not from what is easy.
  • Constructed backend: if pure hand-compiled induction passes its gates, the "compiler emits machine code" claim is proven at toy scale; the follow-up is composing two constructed skills in one model.
The test of the paradigm is not whether experts find Loom elegant — it is whether a person who cannot define "residual stream" can get a model that verifiably does what they asked, and a refusal they can understand when it cannot.
