Programming the transformer

Can the internals of a transformer be treated the way a compiler treats a CPU — with variables, instructions, a debugger, and eventually a language a non-expert can program in? This project answers empirically: five preregistered experiments validate which layers of the hypothetical "abstraction stack" are real, and Loom, a prototype programming layer, is being built on the layers that survived.

The literature synthesis that seeded this project concluded the layer, if it exists, is plural and leaky — not one clean instruction set but several partial descriptions, each with a measurable failure fraction ("leak budget"). That claim was turned into five falsifiable hypotheses with numeric refutation thresholds, then tested on a single NVIDIA GB10 with small transformers whose ground truth we control.

E2 — Circuits: real, discrete, and shortcut-prone

Induction (in-context copying) forms as a phase transition: prefix-matching attention rises to @@E2_SCORE@@ inside @@E2_WF@@ of training, co-timed with the in-context loss collapsing to near zero — and it lives in layer 2 only (@@E2_L2@@ vs @@E2_L1@@ in layer 1), the classic two-layer composition. The route was the finding: the first design used fixed-offset repetition and got perfect copying with zero induction attention — a positional shortcut. Randomizing the gap destroyed the shortcut and the true circuit appeared.

E3 & E4 — Features: descriptive, not canonical

E3 asked whether SAE quality metrics can even tell a trained model from a random one (they failed to in the literature). Here they separated at @@E3_SIG@@σ — but in the wrong direction: the random model reconstructs more easily (FVU @@E3_FR@@ vs @@E3_FT@@) because its activations are low-dimensional. The metric scores reconstructability, not learned structure. E4: on known concepts, a plain logistic probe on raw activations ties the SAE probe on belief-region (@@E4_RB@@ vs @@E4_SB@@) and loses slightly on entropy (@@E4_RE@@ vs @@E4_SE@@) — parity, not superiority, replicated at a gap of 0.001 ± 0.002.

E5 — Weights: undecidable as measured, and that is the result

Training at 80% weight sparsity kept @@E5_ACC@@ accuracy with a perfectly faithful extracted circuit — sparsity is nearly free. But the headline "4.8× smaller circuit" deflated on replication: the weight ratio (@@E5_WR@@), an independent edge-level ratio (@@E5_ER@@), and the imposed sparsity knob (q = @@E5_Q@@) are the same number, with zero variance across seeds. Every size metric we built measures the hyperparameter, not the learned mechanism. The experiment now self-reports this (size_metrics_are_tautological) and its posterior is pinned at the 0.5 prior — passing an uninformative test is not evidence.

4 · What almost fooled us

Every positive result was one control away from being wrong. These four failure modes were each caught live, on systems whose ground truth we knew — which is precisely why toy models first:

The reservoir effect

An untrained network probed belief state at R² 0.887 — random features over recent tokens explain almost everything. Any probe result without an untrained control and a trivial-baseline comparison is unfounded.

The positional shortcut

Perfect in-context copying with zero induction attention: fixed-offset repetition is solvable from positional embeddings alone. A behavioral metric certified a capability the mechanism did not implement.

GPU non-determinism

Identical runs moved a headline accuracy by 7 points (0.786 → 0.857) — enough to flip a verdict. Seeding is not enough: the memory-efficient attention backward pass is non-deterministic; the math backend must be pinned before CUDA initializes.

The tautological metric

"Sparse circuits are 4.8× smaller" was the sparsity knob echoed back. The tell: zero variance across seeds. A result that reproduces perfectly may be reproducing a constant.

Standing rule, now enforced in code: the pipeline flags seed-invariant metrics, refuses to count tautological passes as evidence, verifies that support rules in code match the preregistered thresholds, and never lets the test suite write into real results.

5 · Loom — building the layer

Validation said which layers are load-bearing. Loom is the constructive step: a language in which a person who has never heard of gradients declares what the model must do, and a compiler either delivers a verified model or refuses with a diagnosis. The compiler's contract is the project's central lesson turned into software: no capability ships unverified.

# hello_transformer.weave.yaml — a program, not a config:
# the author never mentions losses, layers, or learning rates

The compiler writes machine code

The constructed backend is the sharpest claim made real: kind: induction compiled directly into weights — zero training, 0.4 seconds. The design is a three-layer program written into attention matrices: two "shift" heads copy the previous and the second-previous token into reserved subspaces of the residual stream, and a third head matches the current two-token context against those buffers — trigram matching, which collapses spurious matches from ~1 per sequence to ~0.06. Two tricks make hand-written weights survive a real transformer: zero-mean Hadamard block codes (so LayerNorm degenerates into a harmless rescale) and near-hard attention via large score scales. The hand-built circuit scores 0.979 on the mechanism metric where the trained model scored 0.607 — with 13,152 nonzero weights (2.8% of the model). The honest trade-off: blunt calibration (0.98 nats vs the trained 0.08) — a perfect mechanism is not yet a calibrated probability model.

The red team, and what it changed

An adversarial agent attacked the paradigm and found five holes — including a critical one: the monitor library silently fitted probes to random labels when ground truth wasn't supplied, reporting confident R² while reading nothing. Exactly the confident-wrong-answer failure this project catalogued in phase one, reproduced inside our own compiler. Three findings are fixed and locked by regression tests (vacuous gates like >-1 refused at parse time; ground truth now mandatory for monitors; controls that would silently do nothing refuse to install). Two are documented as v0 limits: suppression is token-level not concept-level, and gates certify the distribution they were measured on.

When the compiler says no

The curriculum backend's multi-skill build passed induction (0.909 — MLP compensation chosen by the planner improved on the attention-only 0.779) and state-tracking (0.911), but parity stalled at chance through three escalating attempts — consistent with the literature: parity is the canonical SGD-hard function for transformers. So it became a language rule: concept: token_parity now refuses at compile time with a plain-English diagnosis and a suggestion of a compilable alternative. A type error beats a failed 23-minute build. The refusal artifacts are kept as first-class results.

6 · Steering — the open decisions

  • H5 needs a non-weight-counting ruler. Causal edge pruning or description length of the extracted algorithm; until then the weights layer stays at its prior.
  • Scale jump: re-run E3/E4 on a real LM (Pythia-160M) where the random-model control has its intended meaning; port a Loom monitor to it.
  • Loom expressiveness: v0 has 3 skill kinds, 2 control kinds, 1 monitor kind. The next kinds should come from what a real user asks for, not from what is easy.
  • Constructed backend: if pure hand-compiled induction passes its gates, the "compiler emits machine code" claim is proven at toy scale; the follow-up is composing two constructed skills in one model.
The test of the paradigm is not whether experts find Loom elegant — it is whether a person who cannot define "residual stream" can get a model that verifiably does what they asked, and a refusal they can understand when it cannot.
